# State income tax comparison sheet (planner vs. agency calculators)

Generated 2026-10-04 from the Complete Retirement Plan engine at commit b5f6101. Purpose: let a tax professional, or you, check the planner's modeled **2026 state and local income tax** against each state's own calculator or return form for three sample households. This sheet is not tax advice and does not replace a review by someone credentialed.

## How to use it
1. Enter the household below in the state's official calculator, or complete its return form, as a **full-year resident** taking the **standard deduction**.
2. Compare to the planner's figure. Differences of a few dollars are rounding. Larger gaps are the leads to investigate; send me the state, household and the agency's number and I'll trace the line.
3. **Ignore credits** the agency calculator applies on top (earned income, property tax, child, low-income, other-state-tax credits). The planner is a *pre-credit* estimate and leaves them out; a few personal credits it models are noted in each state's consent text.
4. Local taxes (Maryland counties, Indiana counties, Pennsylvania earned income tax) are shown separately where the planner models them.
5. Tips, overtime and car-loan deductions are not modeled and are not in these households.

## Where I would look first (states whose rules most often differ from a simple read)
These are the provisions that were wrong or missing in earlier versions of the modules, so they are the places a mismatch is most likely to show:
- **Pennsylvania, New Jersey:** 401(k) deferrals, IRA deduction and capital-loss deduction are treated differently from federal AGI (Household C).
- **Maryland, Virginia:** personal exemptions and the age deduction (Households A and B).
- **Minnesota:** the Social Security subtraction, age/blind deduction and the standard deduction limit.
- **Missouri, South Carolina, Wisconsin, Montana, New Mexico, North Dakota, Vermont, Hawaii, Massachusetts:** capital-gain preferences (Households B and C).
- **Arizona:** the 25% subtraction for long-term gains on assets acquired after 2011 is **not** modeled (the planner has no acquisition dates); the agency figure will be lower for Households B and C if the gain qualifies.
- **Colorado, Rhode Island:** pension and annuity modifications that now count 401(k) and annuity income (Colorado also IRA income) under the state caps (Households A and B).
- **Arkansas:** 50% of net capital gain is excluded (Household B and C).
- **Kansas, Louisiana, West Virginia, Indiana, Georgia, Idaho, Delaware, Oklahoma, Mississippi:** pension and retirement-income rules by pension type (Household B has a federal government pension; Household A a private one).

## Known reasons the planner will not match an agency calculator exactly
- Credits, itemized deductions and dependents are excluded.
- Military retirement pay, disability-based exemptions and state-specific pension systems the planner cannot identify are excluded (the consent text for each state lists these).
- Later-year amounts are held at 2026 law, not forecast. This sheet is 2026 only.
- Several 2026 amounts were derived from statute because the agency had not yet published them (California, Rhode Island, Vermont and Washington parameters are held from 2025 pending agency publications; see ANNUAL_STATE_REFRESH.md). Expect small differences in those states until the agency figures arrive.
- Federal AGI is computed by the planner's federal engine; if an agency calculator asks for federal AGI or taxable Social Security, use the figures above.
